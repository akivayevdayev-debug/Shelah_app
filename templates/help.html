Asking a good sh'elah

The more specific your question, the more useful the sourced answer. Include the practical situation, not just the topic — "Can I turn on a light with a timer before Shabbat starts" retrieves better sources than "Shabbat rules". If your question depends on where you live or which community's custom you follow, say so; the community-lens selector (below) helps with this too.

Answer modes

Every answer is generated in one of four modes, selectable before you ask:

  • Balanced — the default: a clear, well-rounded answer with cited sources.
  • Practical — leads with the bottom-line halachic conclusion first, detail after.
  • Sources — emphasizes the primary texts themselves, with minimal synthesis commentary.
  • Strict — the most conservative framing, explicitly declining to rule on questions requiring a posek.

Community lens

Many practical questions have different answers by community (Ashkenaz, Sephardic, Yemenite, and others). Selecting a community lens biases retrieval toward that community's customs and sources where relevant — it does not hide other views, and the AI will still say when a practice is genuinely disputed.

Reader features

  • Shul Mode — a larger, auto-scrolling display meant for reading along during services; toggle it from the reader's settings panel.
  • Bookmarks — save any passage or answer for later; signed-in users' bookmarks sync across devices.
  • Export — download a chapter as a document (docx/pdf) for offline study or printing.
  • Cited sources — every AI answer's source list links directly back into the reader at the exact cited passage.

Calendar & zmanim tour

The calendar view shows the Hebrew date, the week's parasha, holidays, and the daily zmanim (halachic times) computed for your location — either detected automatically or set manually if you'd rather not share it. Candle-lighting and havdalah times update automatically as Shabbat and holidays approach.
