AI Disclosure

Last updated: August 15, 2026 · v1.0

← Back to Sh'elah

🤖 You are interacting with an AI system. Every answer on Sh'elah is generated (in whole or in part) by large language models, not written or reviewed by a human before it reaches you. This page explains how that works and what it means for you.

1. Which AI Models We Use

Sh'elah synthesizes answers using commercial large language models from two providers, chosen dynamically for availability and answer quality:

Provider Role
Anthropic Claude Primary synthesis model
Google Gemini Synthesis and translation support

Neither provider trains its general models on your questions through this integration by default; see our Privacy Policy §4/§9 for exactly what is sent to each.

2. How Answers Are Generated (RAG, in Plain Language)

Sh'elah uses a technique called retrieval-augmented generation ('RAG'). In plain terms:

  1. Your question is analyzed and used to search third-party text corpora — primarily Sefaria (Torah, Talmud, halachic codes and commentaries), plus Hebcal for calendar/zmanim facts and, as a last resort when Judaic sources cannot answer, general web sources including Wikipedia.
  2. The most relevant passages found are retrieved and handed to the AI model alongside your question, as grounding context.
  3. The AI model synthesizes those passages into a readable answer, with citations back to the retrieved Sources where possible.

This grounds answers in real texts more than an ungrounded chatbot would, but it does not eliminate error: retrieval can pull the wrong passage, or a passage that is only tangentially related, and the model can still misinterpret or misstate what it retrieved.

3. Limitations & Hallucination Risk

Like all large language models, the AI models behind Sh'elah can 'hallucinate' — state something confidently that is inaccurate, fabricated, or not actually supported by the retrieved Sources. Specific risks include:

  • Misattributed or fabricated citations (a source reference that looks plausible but is wrong or does not exist)
  • Presenting one halachic opinion as if it were the only one, when a genuine machloket (dispute) exists
  • Retrieval mismatches — pulling a passage that is topically related but not actually on point
  • Translation drift when a Hebrew/Aramaic source is rendered into English or vice versa

We reduce these risks with source-grounding, prompt-level guardrails, and a post-generation output check, but we cannot eliminate them. Always verify a citation against the original text (linked from the answer) before relying on it — see our Terms of Service §6.

4. What Sh'elah's AI Does NOT Do

  • It does not issue binding halachic rulings (p'sak halacha).
  • It does not replace a rabbi, posek, or other rabbinic authority.
  • It does not give legal, medical, mental-health, or financial advice.
  • It never claims, and is instructed never to claim, to be a rabbi or a human authority.
  • Medical, legal, mental-health, self-harm, and abuse-adjacent questions are routed to a referral response (a qualified professional + your rabbi), never to a substantive "ruling".

5. Human-in-the-Loop: Always Consult Your Rabbi

Every AI answer is paired with a persistent, non-dismissible-on-first-use disclaimer: this is educational information, not a halachic ruling — consult your rabbi for a final decision. This is not boilerplate; it reflects how the Service is actually designed to be used — as a study aid that helps you find and understand sources faster, with your rabbi as the final decision-maker for anything you intend to act on.

6. Age-Appropriate Output

Because Sh'elah's minimum age is 13 (see our Terms of Service §3), sensitive halachic topics (e.g., family purity, intimacy) are answered with clinical, educational language and source citations, without graphic or explicit detail — the AI is directed to explain the halachic framework and refer you to a rabbi, teacher, or parent for practical specifics. See docs/AGE_AND_SAFETY_POLICY.md for the full policy.
