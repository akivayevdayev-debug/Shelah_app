About Sh'elah

A Torah library and halachic AI assistant, built by one person.

← Back to Sh'elah

Mission

Sh'elah exists to make it easier to look something up — a halacha, a custom, a source, a zman — and to see the primary text behind the answer, not just a summary of it. The library, the calendar, and the AI assistant are all built around the same idea: bring you closer to the sources, in a language and format you can actually use, and never stand between you and your own rabbi's final word.

Who builds it

Sh'elah is built and maintained by a single independent developer — there is no company, no editorial board, and no team of poskim behind it. That's worth knowing plainly: content is assembled from established sources and reviewed as time allows, not vetted by a rabbinic committee before publication. Treat every answer here the way you'd treat a well-sourced reference book, not a rabbinic authority.

How answers are sourced

The library pulls primary texts from Sefaria's open corpus (Tanakh, Talmud, halachic codes, commentaries), Hebcal for the calendar and zmanim, and a curated set of community-custom (minhag) datasets. When you ask a question, the AI assistant retrieves passages from this same library and from whitelisted external references first — a live web search is used only as a last resort, and every synthesized answer is expected to cite the sources it actually drew on.

For the full technical and safety detail on how the AI synthesizes an answer, see the AI Disclosure.

Update cadence

Sh'elah is under active, ongoing development by its one maintainer — features, sources, and fixes ship on an irregular schedule rather than a fixed release calendar. There is no guaranteed uptime or support SLA; see the Terms of Service for the full posture on service availability.

Always ask your rabbi

Nothing on Sh'elah — including anything the AI assistant generates — is a halachic ruling. It is educational information, formatted to help you find and understand primary sources faster. For any real-world question with practical consequences, ask a rabbi qualified to answer it for your specific situation and community.
